1 Overview

Case 31 contains two multi-item scales, each measured on a 5-point agreement scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree):

Show R code 
kable(data.frame(
  Scale = c(rep("R3: Attitude toward employee proficiency", 4),
            rep("R6: Attitude toward operational effectiveness", 4)),
  Item  = names(item_labels),
  Wording = unname(item_labels)
))

Following the assignment, the analysis runs in this order:

  1. Suitability check - KMO and Bartlett’s test of sphericity.
  2. Convergent validity - number of factors (eigenvalues, scree plot, parallel analysis) and the size of the loadings/communalities when each scale is factored on its own.
  3. Discriminant validity - factoring all eight items together to see whether items load on the construct they are supposed to represent.
  4. Reliability - Cronbach’s alpha for each scale.
  5. Implication - whether to build a composite score for each scale.

Factor analysis uses maximum likelihood (fm = "ml") and an oblique rotation (oblimin) when more than one factor is extracted. All 128 respondents have complete answers on all eight items.

2 Suitability for Factor Analysis

Show R code 
suit <- function(x, name) {
  k <- KMO(x)
  b <- cortest.bartlett(cor(x), n = nrow(x))
  data.frame(Scale = name,
             KMO = round(k$MSA, 2),
             `Lowest item MSA` = round(min(k$MSAi), 2),
             `Bartlett chi-sq` = round(b$chisq, 1),
             df = b$df,
             p = format.pval(b$p.value, digits = 3, eps = .001),
             check.names = FALSE)
}

                                                ".05 to 1.00", "No", ".47", "No"),
  check.names = FALSE
))

R3 - Attitude toward employee proficiency (Q3-Q6). The scale is valid and reliable. One factor emerged, all items load strongly with high communalities (convergent validity), the items stay on their own factor when combined with the R6 items (discriminant validity), and alpha = .87 (reliability).

  • Implication: Combine Q3-Q6 into a single composite score (the mean of the four items) and use that composite to address R3 in the next step of the analysis.

R6 - Attitude toward operational effectiveness (Q31-Q34). The scale is neither valid nor reliable as one construct. KMO is at the minimum, the items do not converge on one factor, they do not form their own factor when combined with the R3 items, and alpha = .47.

  • Implication: Do not create a composite score for R6. Q31 (confidentiality), Q32 (loan processing speed), Q33 (services meet needs), and Q34 (simple loan applications) appear to measure different aspects of operations rather than one attitude. Each item should be analyzed separately when addressing R6.


```{r}
#| label: fig-both-diagram
#| fig-cap: "Two-factor solution, all 8 items (loadings below .30 hidden)"

fa.diagram(efa_both, cut = .30, digits = 2,
           main = "Two-Factor Solution: R3 and R6 Items")
```

::: callout-warning
## What this means

- **R3 items:** Q3-Q6 load cleanly on one factor (ML2: .68 to .90) with no meaningful cross-loadings. The employee proficiency construct is distinct from the operational items.
- **R6 items:** They do **not** form their own factor. The second factor (ML1) is driven almost entirely by Q32 (.99), with Q34 (.44) as the only other item above .30. Q31 (.23 / .18) and Q33 (.35 / .08) do not load on the R6 factor at all; Q33 actually leans toward the **employee proficiency** factor.
- **Factor correlation:** The two factors correlate at .39, so they are related but clearly separate.

**Conclusion:** R3 shows **discriminant validity**. R6 does not, because its items do not emerge together on a factor of their own.
:::

## What this means

- **R3:** alpha = .87 (standardized .87), above the .70 standard and in the "good" range. Dropping any item would not raise alpha (the highest alpha-if-dropped is .85), so all four items should stay.
- **R6:** alpha = .47 (standardized .46), far below .70. The average inter-item correlation is only .18. Dropping an item does not fix it: removing Q31, Q33, or Q34 leaves alpha between .44 and .47, and removing Q32 drops it to .14. No combination of these four items reaches an acceptable level.

# Summary and Implications for Data Analysis

```{r}
#| label: tbl-summary
#| tbl-cap: "Summary of validity and reliability results"

kable(data.frame(
  Criterion = c("KMO", "Factors suggested (parallel analysis)", "Loadings (own scale)",
                "Communalities (own scale)", "Separate factor with all 8 items",
                "Cronbach's alpha", "Make a composite scale?"),
  `R3: Employee proficiency (Q3-Q6)` = c(".78", "1", ".73 to .93", ".53 to .86",
                                         "Yes", ".87", "Yes"),
  `R6: Operational effectiveness (Q31-Q34)` = c(".50", "2", ".23 to ~1.0 (only Q32 strong)",
                                                ".05 to 1.00", "No", ".47", "No"),
  check.names = FALSE
))
```

**R3 - Attitude toward employee proficiency (Q3-Q6).** The scale is valid and reliable. One factor emerged, all items load strongly with high communalities (convergent validity), the items stay on their own factor when combined with the R6 items (discriminant validity), and alpha = .87 (reliability).

- **Implication:** Combine Q3-Q6 into a single composite score (the mean of the four items) and use that composite to address R3 in the next step of the analysis.

**R6 - Attitude toward operational effectiveness (Q31-Q34).** The scale is neither valid nor reliable as one construct. KMO is at the minimum, the items do not converge on one factor, they do not form their own factor when combined with the R3 items, and alpha = .47.

- **Implication:** Do **not** create a composite score for R6. Q31 (confidentiality), Q32 (loan processing speed), Q33 (services meet needs), and Q34 (simple loan applications) appear to measure different aspects of operations rather than one attitude. Each item should be analyzed separately when addressing R6.
